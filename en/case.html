Official Ruling of the French Court of Cassation (Criminal Chamber) — 14 February 2024

Definitive Dismissal: "Douma Four" Enforced Disappearance & Torture

Ruling No. 00342 | Appeal No. 23-86.719 | ECLI:FR:CCASS:2024:CR00342 | Official Légifrance Reference

By an irrevocable decree, the French Court of Cassation rejected all appeals filed by civil party NGOs (FIDH, LDH, SCM) and upheld the dismissal of charges (non-lieu) against Majdi Nema regarding complicity in enforced disappearances—namely the abduction of human rights lawyer Razan Zaitouneh, Samira Khalil, Wael Hamadeh, and Nazem Hammadi—along with charges of torture and deliberate attacks on civilians[cite: 11, 16].

The Court noted that none of the allegations were contemporaneous with Nema's presence (having departed Ghouta in May 2013, seven months before the incident)[cite: 14], and that no evidence proved any positive act, instruction, or command authority on his part to facilitate or order such offenses.

Official Syrian Extradition Request & Sovereignty

Universal jurisdiction is by definition subsidiary: it was designed solely as an exceptional remedy because Syrian courts were incapable of delivering justice under the Assad dictatorship[cite: 13].

Following the collapse of the regime in December 2024, the newly established Syrian government—through the Ministry of Justice and Ministry of Foreign Affairs—dispatched an official diplomatic note requesting that France transfer Majdi Nema to Syria to stand trial under his domestic judicial system[cite: 12].

Diplomatic Inaction: The French government has failed to provide any formal response to the Syrian Embassy, retaining custody of a Syrian-Turkish citizen whose native sovereign state has officially claimed jurisdiction, prompting the defense team to file claims for arbitrary detention[cite: 12].
